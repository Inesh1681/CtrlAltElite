01The problem

A complaint gets filed. Then it disappears.

Civic complaints don’t fail at reporting. They fail at the five points after it. Flip the switch to see what changes.

1 · ROUTING

Wrong desk

Roads, garbage and lights sit with the city corporation, water with BWSSB, power with BESCOM, signals with Traffic Police. Residents guess.

11 problem types map to the right department and corporation automatically, and send through its official channel in one tap.

2 · DUPLICATES

A hundred tickets for one pothole

Every neighbour files separately. The city sees noise, not a signal.

Reports of the same problem within 50 m merge into one issue. More reports raise its priority instead of the pile.

3 · PRIORITY

First come, first served

A sparking wire outside a school waits behind a faded road marking.

A transparent 0–100 score: severity, public demand, overdue days, location risk and category. Officials can see why it ranks where it does.

4 · FOLLOW-UP

Nobody chases it

Fix windows pass quietly. Residents give up.

Every complaint runs on a clock: follow-up, escalation to the named officer, then a one-tap RTI application.

5 · CLOSURE

“Closed” isn’t “fixed”

Tickets get closed without anyone checking the street.

Verified fixed only with on-site before and after photos, city approval and the resident’s confirmation.

Result today: the loop never closes.

06Trust

Nobody marks their own homework.

A contractor can’t close their own job. “Verified fixed” needs independent sign-off, and a bad fix has consequences.

STEP 1

Contractor submits proof

Before and after photos, both within 100 m, time-stamped.

AND

City approves

The official checks the photos, or sends it back as not fixed.

AND

A resident confirms

Or 72 hours pass with no dispute from anyone who reported it.

IF NOT

Reopened

Back to the same contractor at higher priority, and it counts against their reopen rate.

Verified fixed = proof + city approval + (resident confirms or 72 h, no dispute)
