Civic tech · Bengaluru · SDG 11

Getting a complaint filed is easy. Getting it fixed is the hard part.

UrbOasis is the follow-through layer for civic complaints. It routes each problem to the right department and city corporation, sends the complaint in one tap through the department’s official channel, and chases it with follow-ups, escalation letters and RTI applications until it’s fixed.

Problem analysis

Civic complaints don’t fail at reporting. They fail after it.

Follow a pothole from the moment someone notices it. Tap each stage to see where it breaks down, and what UrbOasis does about it.

Fragmented ownership

Roads, garbage and streetlights sit with GBA, water and sewage with BWSSB, power with BESCOM, signals with Traffic Police. BBMP’s 2025 split into GBA and five city corporations added more confusion.

No follow-through

Helplines take complaints, but nobody tells the citizen when a complaint is overdue, who to escalate to, or what to write. Most people give up after one try.

No shared evidence

Every complaint lives alone. Nobody can show that a ward’s potholes take three weeks, so there’s no pressure to improve.

Why now

Bengaluru’s civic system has just changed shape

Three shifts make a follow-through layer more useful now than ever.

BBMP became GBA and five corporations

In 2025, BBMP’s civic work moved to the Greater Bengaluru Authority and five new city corporations: Central, North, South, East and West.

Residents don’t know who owns their street

New boundaries and new offices mean more complaints at the wrong desk. UrbOasis suggests the likely corporation from the pin and addresses letters to it.

Corporation elections are due

Elections to the new city corporations are due by December 2026. Ward-level evidence of what gets fixed, and how fast, will matter to residents and candidates.

One loop, three users

From “that’s broken” to “verified fixed”, with everyone who touches the problem

Residents report. The city sees one ranked queue instead of a pile of duplicates. Contractors prove the fix on site. The resident has the final say.

Routes and sends

Maps 11 problem types to the right department and city corporation, then sends it in one tap through a channel published on the department’s own site.

Legal escalation

Turns the RTI Act’s 30-day reply into a one-tap application with the five questions that get a written answer.

Collective pressure

A public community board where residents vote, one vote per Google account, plus co-sign links and society reports. One complaint becomes a ranked list officials can see.

Private by design

No account and no server. Photos are re-saved without location metadata. Built with the DPDP Act 2023 in mind.

Competition

We don’t replace official channels. We help people use them well.

Every complaint still goes through the official route, so it lands on the department’s record.

Sahaaya 2.0 and helplines

The official way to register a complaint, built to receive and route it inside government. UrbOasis sends people there, then helps them chase it.

GBA’s announced single-window system

GBA has announced a single window for civic complaints. If it offers a public interface, UrbOasis can plug into it to file and track automatically.

Social media and civic portals

Good for visibility, weak on follow-through. UrbOasis writes the public post and keeps the timeline a post alone loses.

The pitch

Bengaluru doesn’t need another place to complain. It needs complaints that get fixed.

UrbOasis gives every resident and housing society a clear path from “that’s broken” to “it’s fixed”, and turns their complaints into evidence that holds departments to account.

Problem
Complaints reach the wrong department and nobody follows up, so they quietly die.
Solution
Automatic routing, one-tap sending through each department’s official channel, and a follow-up, escalation and RTI engine that chases it.
Customer
Residents, who report and vote, and the housing societies and RWAs that chase civic problems every month.
Model
Free for residents (drives adoption and data). Societies and RWAs pay: a free 3-month pilot, then ₹999 per society per month, or custom pricing for federations. Later, a B2G pilot tier for city corporations (City Console and Contractor app), priced per pilot.
Edge
Built for follow-through rather than filing. Once society boards launch, pooled fix times by ward and department become evidence that is hard to get today.
Impact
SDG 11 and 16: safer streets, basic services that work, and more accountable local government.
